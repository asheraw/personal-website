# Story frameworks

Asher wants his content built on one of these four frameworks, confirmed directly: *"there's no life, no story, no use of structure like the PAS, Story Spine, 3-Act, or Hero's Journey (which are the 4 frameworks I want my content to always be based on)."* Pick the one that fits the material before drafting — don't just list beats and hope the shape emerges.

The common failure these all guard against: skipping straight from problem to resolution. Every framework below has a middle section (agitate / rising action / trials) that must not be compressed — that's where the actual story lives.

## PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solve

Best for: a practical frustration and a change in tools, habits, or decisions. Classic for workflow/tool posts.

- **Problem**: state the frustration or tension plainly.
- **Agitate**: don't rush past this. Dwell on why it's frustrating, what it cost, what kept it going, what almost happened instead. This is the section most likely to get compressed by accident — resist that.
- **Solve**: the change, and what it actually produced.

Real example: "Confession: I Taught AI for Months Before I Ever Paid for It." Problem: defaults to free tools even as an AI trainer. Agitate: the GrooveFunnels trauma behind the subscription aversion, the drawn-out wait for Kimi K3 that never released, the discomfort of the contradiction. Solve: buying Claude Pro and getting a day's worth of progress that outpaced months of free-tool tinkering.

## Story Spine

Best for: a chronological account with one clear inciting incident and a chain of consequences. Pixar's structure: *Once upon a time... Every day... Until one day... Because of that... Because of that... Until finally... And ever since then...*

Real example: "How I Lost My Writing Home." Once upon a time he wrote almost daily. Every day was article marketing, syndication, momentum. Until one day GrooveFunnels arrived promising to replace WordPress. Because of that he moved everything over. Because of that the platform's bugs and a mishandled affiliate commission made logging in feel like ick, so he stopped writing entirely for years. Until finally he decided to vibe-code his own site. And ever since then he's been writing and publishing again.

Don't skip the cascading "because of that" links — that chain is what makes years of silence feel earned rather than sudden.

## 3-Act structure

Best for: a general default when nothing more specific fits, or a bounded event (a rehearsal, a decision, a single production).

- **Act 1 — ordinary world + inciting incident**: where things stood, then what changed.
- **Act 2 — rising complication**: the struggle, the attempts, the setbacks. This is the longest act and should not be thin.
- **Act 3 — resolution**: not necessarily a fix — see the main SKILL.md on endings not needing to be tidy. What changed, what it means now.

Confirmed directly as a shape Asher wants: a reviewed draft was praised specifically because *"it also wraps the beginning and the end, it has a 3-act arc to it, so following frameworks makes it easy to follow."*

## Hero's Journey

Best for: a bigger reflective post about a real turning point — heavier than the others, so don't force it onto a short post about a minor event.

Real example: "Easter 2018: Under the Sycamore Tree." Call to adventure: casting as the lead. Trials: the physical preparation (hair, weight loss via ketosis, adjusting body language across three shows), technical near-disasters handled live on stage. Ordeal: the ketosis collapse backstage before the final show, praying alone holding the cross. Return transformed: the reflection on surrender, on preparation mattering less than release, on why the story's outcome (Marion staying dead) mattered theologically. This is the richest example in the corpus of slowing down for the pivotal scene (the collapse) rather than summarizing it.

## Picking one

Ask what kind of post this is:
- A decision or workflow change with a clear before/after → **PAS**.
- A slow decline or slow return over real calendar time → **Story Spine**.
- A single bounded event (a show, a rehearsal, a trip) → **3-Act**.
- A major personal or spiritual turning point worth real weight → **Hero's Journey**.

If genuinely unsure, 3-Act is the safe default — it's the shape underneath the other three anyway.
