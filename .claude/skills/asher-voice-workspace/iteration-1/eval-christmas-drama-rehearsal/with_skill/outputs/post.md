# Confession: I Didn't Get the Lead This Christmas

Truth be told, I auditioned for a lead role in this year's Christmas drama. Cast list went up, and I was a side character.

A bit disappointing, not gonna lie.

## So What Do You Do With a Side Character?

A side character doesn't get much from the script. A handful of scenes, one line that actually matters, and that's about it. No backstory, no arc, nothing that tells you who this person is.

So I built one myself.

Who was this person before they walked into the scene? What did they want? What were they carrying that the script never bothers to mention? Nobody asked me to do this. I just started doing it anyway, mostly so the one line I had would come from somewhere real instead of just being said.

And honestly, I got way more into it than I expected to. More than I was into the idea of the lead.

## What Happens When You Forget Your One Line?

Dress rehearsal. My one line was gone. Completely gone.

I froze for a second, that horrible blank kind of second, and then just said something. Not the scripted line. Something that came out of all that backstory I'd been quietly building for weeks, because apparently that's what was actually sitting in my head instead of the script.

It worked. Genuinely worked better than the line that was written.

The director laughed about it afterward and told me to just keep it in.

## Turns Out the Lead Wasn't the Point

I still think about the lead I didn't get. But if I'd gotten it, I don't think I'd have spent weeks figuring out who my character really was outside that one line. And I definitely wouldn't have had anything real to reach for the second I forgot it.

The role I didn't want ended up giving me the one moment from this whole production I'll actually remember.
