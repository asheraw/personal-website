# I Almost Didn't Buy Claude Pro. Here's Why That's Embarrassing.

I teach AI classes for a living. I stand in front of people and explain how these tools work, why they're worth using, how to get the most out of them. And for months, I refused to pay $20 a month for the best one.

Let me explain how that happened, because the reason isn't really about AI at all.

## The GrooveFunnels scar tissue

A while back I signed up for GrooveFunnels, a CMS platform that promised to replace WordPress. It didn't. It was buggy in ways that cost me real time, and at one point it even messed up an affiliate commission I was owed. Not a huge amount of money in the grand scheme of things, but enough to leave a mark.

After that, I went cold turkey. Not just on GrooveFunnels — on basically every software subscription. If it recurred monthly, I didn't want it. I didn't trust the model anymore: pay upfront, hope the thing works, hope the company doesn't quietly shortchange you somewhere down the line.

That reflex stuck around a lot longer than the GrooveFunnels situation itself. It became a default setting. Free tools first, always. Paid tools only if there was genuinely no other option. And even then, I'd hesitate.

## Building a website on free AI, on principle

When I started rebuilding my website, that default kicked in immediately. I was using GLM, the free model from z.ai, to do the work. It's a real model, it can genuinely help you build things, and it cost me nothing.

And I kept telling myself I was one step away from not even needing that. There was an open-source model, Kimi K3, that hadn't released yet. My plan was simple: wait for it, run it myself or use it for free, and never have to put a credit card down for AI help at all. I teach people how to use these tools, and I was quietly proud of the fact that I wasn't paying for any of them myself.

Kimi K3 never opened up to consumers. I waited, and the free option I was counting on just didn't materialize.

## The day I finally paid for Claude Pro

So I gave in and got Claude Pro.

Within the first day, I had built a proper CMS, backups, and a blog page — more progress on my website than I'd made in months of working with the free tools. Not a slight improvement. Not "well, this is a bit smoother." An entire day undid a backlog that had been sitting untouched for months.

That's the part that actually stings a little, in a good way. It wasn't that the free tools were useless — GLM helped me get real work done, and I don't regret using it. It's that I'd built an entire policy, "never pay for recurring software," out of one bad experience with one company, and then applied that policy uniformly to everything, including tools that had nothing to do with GrooveFunnels and nothing to do with why GrooveFunnels failed me.

## What I'm taking from this

I still don't trust subscriptions by default, and I don't think that instinct is wrong. Getting burned once is a good reason to be careful. But there's a difference between being careful and refusing to evaluate something on its own terms because it shares a billing model with something that hurt you once.

I teach AI for a living, and I almost let a CMS platform from years ago talk me out of using the best tool for the job. Lesson noted. Card on file.
