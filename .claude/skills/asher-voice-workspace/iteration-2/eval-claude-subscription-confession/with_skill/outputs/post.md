# Confession: I Almost Didn't Buy Claude Pro

I teach AI classes for a living. People pay me to explain how these tools work, which ones are actually worth using, how to get real output out of them instead of just poking at a chat box. And for years, I was the one person in that room who wouldn't pay for any of it myself.

That's not a joke I'm making about myself for effect. It's just true. I built my new website almost entirely on free tools, held out on an open-source model that never showed up, and only caved and bought Claude Pro because I genuinely ran out of other options. Here's how that happened.

## What GrooveFunnels Did To Me

Years ago, I signed up for a platform called GrooveFunnels. It promised to replace WordPress outright. One system instead of five, everything under one roof, none of the usual plugin juggling. That was the pitch, and I believed it.

It didn't replace WordPress. It just added a new set of problems next to the old ones. The platform was buggy, and not in the small cosmetic way you can shrug off. And at some point, it mishandled an affiliate commission I was owed. Not a huge amount of money in the grand scheme of things. But it was my money, sitting inside a platform I'd already stopped trusting, and that combination did it.

I went cold turkey. Not just on GrooveFunnels, on basically every recurring software subscription I had. If it charged me monthly, I found the free version, or I found a way to live without it entirely. That wasn't a phase that faded after a few weeks. It stuck. Years later, it was still my default setting: free first, always, and paid only when there was truly no way round it.

## So Why Was I Still Building On Free Tools?

That's a bit much when you actually sit with it. I teach AI classes for a living, and I was applying that exact same cold-turkey rule to AI tools too.

When I started rebuilding my website this year, I built it almost entirely on GLM, the free model over at z.ai. It's a capable tool, and it got me real work done. But the whole time, I was also waiting on something else. There was an open-source model called Kimi K3 that hadn't released to consumers yet, and my plan was to hold out for it. Once it opened up, I'd have something free and mine to run, and I'd never have to think about a subscription for AI again.

So I waited. Kept half an eye on it, session after session, still chipping away at the website on GLM in the meantime, still telling myself the free option was coming.

It didn't come. Kimi K3 never opened up to consumers. I don't know if it still will one day. I just know that at some point, the waiting had gone on longer than the actual GrooveFunnels burn that started it, and there was nothing left to wait for.

## Then I Did The Thing I'd Spent Years Avoiding

So I opened Claude, and I paid for Pro.

After years of cold turkey, that should have been simple maths. It wasn't. It was the first recurring charge I'd willingly signed up for since GrooveFunnels, and I went in half expecting the same story to repeat itself, a big promise, some real bugs, my money and my patience both worse off for it.

That's not what happened.

Within the first day, I had a proper CMS built, an actual system for managing the site instead of hand-editing files and hoping. Then backups, set up properly, the kind of safety net that would have actually meant something back when GrooveFunnels was mishandling my money and I had no way to just roll any of it back. Then a blog page, working, publishable, not a placeholder I'd get around to later. And then more. One piece after another, clicking into place faster than the free tools had ever let me move.

Months. One day.

I'm not exaggerating that comparison to make a point. It's genuinely what happened. Months of chipping away on GLM, waiting on Kimi K3, telling myself I was being sensible about it. Then one day with the paid tool, and I'd built more of my own website than in that entire stretch combined.

## Was Cold Turkey Worth It?

I still don't think going cold turkey after GrooveFunnels was the wrong call. Getting burned once, especially over money that was actually owed to me, is a reasonable enough reason to be careful about what you hand your card details to next.

But that carefulness went on for years, and it got applied to a completely different company, a completely different product, doing a completely different job. GrooveFunnels burned me on a CMS platform. Claude Pro was never that. I just treated it like it might be, because somewhere along the way I'd stopped judging tools on their own terms and started judging them on whether they had a monthly charge attached.

I got burned once, and spent years making sure it never happened again. Turns out all that carefulness ended up costing me something too. Not money this time. Months.

I still teach AI for a living. I just also pay for it now, which, considering the job, is probably how it should have been all along.

P.S. If any of my students are reading this and doing the maths on how long their AI trainer went without paying for AI, yes. I know. I know.
