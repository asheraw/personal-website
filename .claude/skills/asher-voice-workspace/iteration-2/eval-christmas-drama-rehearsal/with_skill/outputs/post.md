# Confession: I Wasn't the Lead, and the Best Line Wasn't Even in the Script

I auditioned for a lead role in this year's church Christmas drama. I did not get it.

Cast list went up on the noticeboard. I scrolled straight to the lead, and my name wasn't there. It was two lines down, next to a part I'd barely registered when I first read through the script.

A bit disappointing, if I'm honest. That particular flavour of "oh" that sits in your chest for the rest of the day. I'd pictured myself carrying scenes, not standing at the edge of them.

## What Do You Do With a Character Who Barely Has Lines?

Side characters don't get much. A handful of scenes, one line that actually matters, and the rest is standing where you're told to stand, reacting to someone else's moment. The script doesn't tell you who this person was before they walked in, what they wanted, what they were carrying. It just needs them there.

So I decided to work it out myself.

Nobody asked me to. There was no rehearsal exercise for it, no director's note asking for it. I just started asking the questions the script never bothered to answer: where had this person come from, what did they actually want in this scene, what were they holding onto under the one line they got to say. I built the whole thing across a couple of weeks of rehearsals, piecing it together in the gaps between blocking, while half the cast was still fumbling their entrances.

And honestly, I got into it more than I expected to. More than I think I would have gotten into the lead, truthfully. There's something about building a whole person out of almost nothing that a fully written role doesn't ask of you. The lead already comes with an arc handed to them. I had to go find mine.

Then came dress rehearsal, and the ten seconds I'll probably remember longest out of this whole run.

## So What Happens When the One Line You Have Just... Isn't There?

Dress rehearsal. Full costume, full lights, director watching from three rows back with a clipboard. I got on stage, hit my mark, waited for my cue.

It came. I opened my mouth.

And... nothing.

Blank. Completely blank. This wasn't the "give me a second, I know this" kind of blank, it was the kind where you can feel there's genuinely nothing there. One line. I only had one. And it was gone.

I froze. Just for a second, but it felt a lot longer than a second, the kind of pause where you can feel the room start to notice. My head was going in about six directions at once, scrabbling through the script, through the blocking notes, through absolutely anything, and coming up with nothing where the line was supposed to be.

And then, underneath all that scrabbling, the weeks of who-is-this-person, what-do-they-want, what-are-they-carrying were still there. Not the line. But the person.

So I said that instead.

The scripted line wasn't it, and neither was anything I'd planned or written down beforehand. What came out of my mouth came from the backstory I'd built for myself, not from anything on the page. It just showed up, in the moment, because apparently that was what was actually sitting in my head, not the script I'd been handed weeks earlier.

It worked. Genuinely worked, and not in a "phew, we survived that" way. It landed harder than the line that was actually written. It fit the character better than the words the script had given them.

The director stopped the scene, laughed, and told me to keep it in.

## Was the Side Character the Real Lead All Along?

I still think about the role I didn't get. That disappointment doesn't fully go away, and I'm not going to pretend it does.

But I don't think I'd have built any of that backstory if I'd gotten the lead. The lead comes with a script that already tells you who you are. I had to go looking for mine. And the only reason I had anything to reach for in that blank, echoing second on stage was because I'd already gone looking for it, weeks before it mattered.

The part of the script that was supposed to sit in the background ended up being the one moment from this whole production I'll actually remember. Funny how that works. I didn't get the lead. Turns out the background had the best line in the show anyway, and it wasn't even the one that was written.

P.S. If anyone from this year's cast is reading this before the run's even over... yes, that line is staying in. You're welcome.
