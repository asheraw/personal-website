## Framework chosen and why

**3-Act structure.** The material is a single bounded event (this year's rehearsal run, casting through dress rehearsal), which the framework guide names as the direct fit ("a single bounded event (a show, a rehearsal, a trip) -> 3-Act"). It's also the framework Asher explicitly praised in his direct feedback on a different draft ("it also wraps the beginning and the end, it has a 3-act arc to it, so following frameworks makes it easy to follow"), and the guide flags it as the safe default when in doubt. Hero's Journey was considered and rejected as too heavy for a single rehearsal anecdote rather than a major turning point; PAS didn't fit because there's no tool/workflow decision; Story Spine didn't fit because there's no multi-stage chronological chain of consequences, just one event with a before/during/after.

Mapped onto the material:
- **Act 1 (ordinary world + inciting incident):** auditioning for the lead, the cast list going up, the disappointment of getting the side role instead.
- **Act 2 (rising complication, the longest act):** deciding to build a backstory for the character nobody asked him to build one for, getting more invested in it than expected, then the dress-rehearsal scene itself - forgetting the one line, the freeze, the racing thoughts, the improvised line landing better than the script, the director laughing and keeping it in.
- **Act 3 (resolution, not necessarily tidy):** reflecting on still missing the lead role while recognising the side role gave him something the lead couldn't, closing on a callback to the "background"/"script" image set up in Act 2.

## Key decisions

- **Dramatized the pivotal scene in six separate beats** rather than the one-paragraph compression from iteration 1: stepping on stage and hitting the mark, the cue arriving, the blank itself (with its own beat), the freeze and racing thoughts, the backstory surfacing under the panic, and the line landing plus the director's reaction as a distinct final beat. This directly answers the feedback quoted in the skill about wanting "Dress rehearsal time... aaaaannd... blank... a thousand emotions running rampant" rather than a summary sentence.
- **Did not invent the actual improvised line, the character's name, the play's title, or any other cast member.** The task said not to invent facts beyond what's given. I dramatized the *feeling and mechanics* of the moment (freezing, racing thoughts, the backstory surfacing) since that's implied scene-setting for any stage performance, but kept the content of what he actually said unspecified ("Whatever came out of my mouth came out because of the backstory..."), matching how Asher's own quoted example also never states the actual replaced words.
- **Used "Drama Ministry"-adjacent generic church-drama vocabulary** but avoided inventing the specific play or Bible story, since that wasn't given.
- **Built the callback ending around "background"/"script"** (a side character is "background" in the script; the improvised line, not the written one, became the one people remember) - an image established in Act 2's opening ("the rest is standing where you're told to stand... it just needs them there") and paid off in the final paragraph, per the skill's rule that a callback reuses a concrete image already planted rather than inventing a new closing metaphor.
- **Left the ending genuinely unresolved on the disappointment** ("I still think about the role I didn't get. That disappointment doesn't fully go away, and I'm not going to pretend it does.") rather than manufacturing a tidy "I'm so glad I didn't get the lead" resolution, per the skill's note that endings don't need a clean fix.
- **Question-headers**: used three, all phrased as questions ("What Do You Do With a Character Who Barely Has Lines?", "So What Happens When the One Line You Have Just... Isn't There?", "Was the Side Character the Real Lead All Along?"), matching the confirmed pattern rather than flattening them into declaratives.
- **Open-loop transition line** before the dress-rehearsal section ("Then came dress rehearsal, and the ten seconds I'll probably remember longest out of this whole run.") - directly modelled on the confirmed-by-feedback pattern in voice-examples.md.
- **Mechanics**: no em dashes anywhere (checked with a grep pass); British/Singapore spelling throughout (flavour, etc.) with a grep check for common American forms (organize/realize/recognize/analyze/customize/favor/color/behavior) turning up none; honesty-marker adverbs (honestly, genuinely, truthfully) kept per the skill's override of no-ai-slop's default instinct to cut them.

## Running through no-ai-slop

Invoked the no-ai-slop skill in detect mode (not rewrite) with explicit instructions not to flag fragments, ellipses, question-headers, em dashes, or the callback ending, since asher-voice's overrides supersede no-ai-slop's defaults on those. Reviewing the draft myself against the loaded no-ai-slop checklist, found and fixed three genuine generic-AI patterns that were NOT covered by asher-voice's protections:

1. **Binary "not X, but Y" contrast**: "Not sulking-in-the-car-park disappointed, but that particular flavour of 'oh'..." - cut the negation, stated the feeling directly.
2. **Vague scene-transition abstraction**: "underneath all that scrabbling, something else was still there" - "something else" is exactly the kind of vague transition the skill warns against ("something shifted"). Replaced with the concrete referent (the backstory work itself).
3. **Negative-listing pattern**: "Not the scripted line. Not anything I'd planned or written down beforehand." - matched no-ai-slop's flagged "Not a X. Not a Y." construction. Rewrote as a direct statement that keeps the same meaning without the listing crutch.

Also checked for and confirmed absent: banned buzzwords, weasel attribution, importance puffery, colon-reveal drama, faux-insight setups, synonym cycling, summary-recap endings, and formatting slop (no bullets, no emoji, headers appropriately sized). The callback ending was deliberately kept as-is since it pays off an image already planted in the post rather than inventing a new closing metaphor.
