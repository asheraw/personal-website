# Confession: I Keep Restarting the Same Habit, and It Keeps Dying on Day Four

Roughly ten times this year, I have woken up early to pray and journal before work. Roughly ten times, by around day four, I have quietly stopped.

I say "roughly" because I genuinely stopped counting properly somewhere along the way. That's its own small admission. You'd think a habit worth restarting ten times would be a habit worth tracking properly, and I didn't even do that.

I'm not writing this because I've fixed it. I haven't. I'm writing it because I think I've finally noticed the shape of the thing, and noticing feels like the only honest place to start.

## Same Door, Every Time

Here's the part that took me embarrassingly long to see, given how many times it's happened: it's never a random Tuesday that kicks the habit off. It's always right after something spiritually big. A church retreat. A weekend where the worship and the people and the whole rhythm of the days crack something open, where God feels close in a way that's hard to manufacture on a normal Thursday.

I come home from those weekends and I mean it. Early alarm. Bible open before the laptop. Journal instead of the phone. I've meant it every single time, which is almost worse than not meaning it, because it means the restart isn't the problem. Something after the restart is.

And for a few days, it actually works.

## What the First Four Days Feel Like

The first morning is easy, almost suspiciously easy. The alarm goes off and I'm up, because the retreat is still close enough to touch, and getting up early to sit with God still feels like the natural continuation of the weekend rather than a new discipline I'm imposing on myself. Day two, still riding it. Day three takes a bit more effort, but I'm still going, and I usually tell someone by then, which in hindsight is probably me trying to lock the habit in with a bit of accountability. Or maybe just wanting credit for it while it still feels good to talk about.

By day four I still believe this is the one that sticks.

Then the actual week starts. Not the retreat-week version of a week, where everything felt lighter because my head was still somewhere else. The real one. Meetings booked early. Messages waiting from the night before. The ordinary noise that was always going to be there, and that the retreat never actually removed, just muffled for a few days.

## Then Came the Morning I Actually Noticed

There's one of these mornings I keep coming back to, because "the habit faded" undersells what it's actually like from the inside.

The alarm goes off in the dark. Same time as the four mornings before it. My hand finds the phone before my brain finds a reason. And there's this half-second where I know exactly what's happening, I've been here nine times before, and I reach for snooze anyway. Not because I decide to, not really. It's more automatic than that, more like the arm moving before the thought finishes. Ten more minutes becomes the whole window gone, because by the time I'm properly awake the first meeting is already close enough that getting up "late" and praying for a rushed fifteen minutes feels pointless. So I don't even try to salvage it. I just get up, get dressed, and get on with the day like the last four mornings never happened.

Nothing dramatic happens in that moment. That's actually the worst part of it. There's no crisis I could point to afterwards, no single bad decision to blame. Just a slightly heavier arm reaching for a slightly closer phone. Then a week goes by, and I realise on a Sunday that I haven't opened the journal since Tuesday, and that the retreat that started all this already feels like it happened to a slightly different version of me.

## So What Am I Actually Chasing?

I've sat with this pattern enough times now, ten reps of the same failure will do that to you eventually, that I've started asking a different question. Not "why can't I keep a habit," because clearly I can keep habits. Plenty of unhelpful ones have stuck around for years without needing a single retreat to kick them off. The question that actually goes somewhere is: what was I trying to get back, each of those ten times?

And I think the honest answer is that I wasn't trying to build a sustainable morning practice. I was trying to recreate a feeling. The retreat gives you a specific kind of closeness. Worship without the usual distractions pulling at you. People around you all leaning the same direction for once. A schedule built entirely around noticing God, with nothing else competing for the slot. That closeness is real. I'm not saying it isn't. But it isn't available on an ordinary weekday morning with a deadline already sitting in my inbox, and I think what I was calling "commitment to pray more" was, at least some of those ten times, actually homesickness for the retreat wearing the costume of discipline.

That's not nothing, working that out. But I don't think it's the fix either, and I want to be honest that I don't have the fix. I don't have a tidy plan for making day five feel like day one. If I wrote one of those right now, I'd just be doing the same thing I do right after every retreat, reaching for the version of this that feels resolved because resolved feels better than uncertain.

What I do have is a slightly different question sitting in my head when the alarm goes off now. Not "can I keep this streak going" but "am I building something for an ordinary Tuesday, or am I still trying to get back to the retreat." I don't know yet what that changes. Maybe nothing, this time. Maybe it's what gets me past day four an eleventh time.

I'll let you know if I make it there. Genuinely don't know yet.
