- Keeps committing to waking early to pray and journal before work.
- Does it for about 4 days straight, then stops.
- This has happened roughly 10 times this year.
- The pattern always starts right after a spiritually "high" moment, e.g. right after a church retreat.
- Normal life routine kicks back in within about a week and the habit fades.
- He hasn't fully figured out the fix.

## Framework choice: PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solve)

Considered against the material:
- **Story Spine** fits a single chronological arc with one inciting incident. This material is a *repeating* cycle (ten instances across the year), which doesn't map cleanly onto a single "once upon a time... and ever since then" chain without either picking one instance and pretending it's the only one, or forcing a false single resolution the material doesn't have.
- **Hero's Journey** is flagged in its own reference doc as "heavier - don't force it onto a short post about a minor event." A recurring habit-relapse pattern is real but not a major single turning point on the scale of the "Easter 2018" example, and forcing that scale onto it risks overstating the material.
- **3-Act** was the safe default per the framework doc, and honestly overlaps a lot with what I ended up writing (ordinary world / rising complication / resolution is baked into any of these).
- **PAS** was the best explicit fit: this is a recurring *practical frustration* (a habit that won't stick) with an identifiable Agitate section (why it keeps happening, what it costs, the specific morning it breaks) and a Solve section that is honestly a partial, unresolved one, not a fixed workflow change. The framework doc's own PAS example (the AI-training/Claude Pro post) has a clean Solve; this post doesn't, and that's fine per the "endings don't need to be tidy" rule, but the shape underneath is still Problem, then real Agitation, then an honest attempt at a Solve that admits it isn't one yet.

Chosen: **PAS**, with the pivotal scene (one representative morning where the streak breaks) dramatized in near-real-time inside the Agitate section, per the skill's explicit instruction not to compress the turning-point moment into a summary sentence.

## Key decisions

- **The realisation had to be earned, not stated as a one-liner.** This was the single most important constraint given the backstory (Asher's direct feedback on "that's the part I keep missing"). I built two full sections (the anatomy of the first four days, then the dramatized morning it breaks) before the piece ever states the "chasing the feeling, not building the habit" idea, and even then I hedged it ("That's not nothing, working that out. But I don't think it's the fix either") rather than presenting it as a solved insight, and explicitly refused to manufacture a fix afterward.
- **No invented fix.** The task explicitly says not to fabricate a tidy resolution. The Solve section states plainly that there isn't one yet, and the closing line ("I'll let you know if I make it there. Genuinely don't know yet.") deliberately leaves it open rather than inventing a "the answer was X" ending.
- **Dramatized pivotal scene is a representative composite, flagged here rather than silently decided.** The user gave the abstract pattern (retreat high, ~4 days, ~10 times, fades within a week) but not a blow-by-blow account of any single morning. Per the skill's explicit instruction to dramatize the turning point in near-real-time rather than summarize it, I wrote one representative "morning it breaks" scene (alarm, reaching for the phone, hitting snooze) built from the pattern Asher described, not from a specific morning he recounted. I deliberately avoided inventing anything that could read as a claimed fact outside the given pattern - no invented job details, no invented people, no invented specific retreat name or location, no invented wake-up time (an earlier draft had "half past five," which I cut on review because it was a fabricated specific number, not something given). This is the one place where I made a judgement call rather than asking first, per Auto Mode; flagging it here as the ambiguous spot per the skill's step 5.
- **Numbers used are only the real ones given**: "roughly ten times," "day four," "about a week." No invented statistics.
- **British/Singapore spelling** applied throughout (realise, journalling), no em dash anywhere (checked with a grep for "—" plus common American spellings after drafting - none found), no American idioms.
- **Title uses the "Confession:" framing** that recurs across Asher's real titles, since this is a confessional admission-of-a-repeated-failure post.
- **Headers**: mixed declarative and question-style, matching the corpus (not every header is forced into a question).
